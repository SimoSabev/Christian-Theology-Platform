# B — Phase 1: Research & Content Foundation

**Mode:** "The Way" — Experience Mode
**Output:** Research notes embedded directly into data files (Phase 2)

> This phase produces no code. It produces the raw theological content that all subsequent phases depend on. A coding agent can skip this phase — content will be provided as data in Phase 2 files. A human researcher or content-writing agent should complete this first.

---

## Research Tasks

### R-01 — Prayer Typology

**Goal:** Understand the 6 types of prayer this platform will teach.

**Sources to synthesize:**
- Thomas Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* II-II, Q.83 (prayer as an act of religion)
- Richard Foster, *Celebration of Discipline* (ch. on prayer)
- Philokalia Vol. 1 — Evagrius on pure prayer

**Output format for each prayer type:**
```
Type: [name]
Definition: [1 sentence, no jargon]
Scripture basis: [1-2 verses]
How to do it: [3-4 step guide]
Starter prayer: [50 words, first-person, simple]
Tradition: [which Christian traditions emphasize this]
```

### R-02 — Lord's Prayer Commentary

**Goal:** Verse-by-verse commentary on Matthew 6:9-13 (8 phrases).

**Sources:**
- N.T. Wright, *The Lord and His Prayer* (1996)
- John Chrysostom, *Homilies on Matthew* 19-20
- Origen, *On Prayer* (Peri Euches)
- Greek interlinear for each phrase

**Output format per phrase:**
```
Greek: [original text]
Transliteration: [phonetic]
Translation variants: [KJV | ESV | NIV rendering]
Theological note: [100 words — what this phrase reveals about God]
Reflection question: [1 question for personal application]
```

### R-03 — Lectio Divina

**Goal:** Accurate, usable guide to the ancient practice.

**Sources:**
- Guigo II, *Ladder of Monks* (Scala Claustralium, c. 1150) — defines the 4 steps
- Benedictine guides (modern)

**Output:**
- 4-step guide (Read, Meditate, Pray, Contemplate) with exact instructions per step
- A suitable practice passage (recommend John 1:1-14 or Psalm 23)
- Timing guidance (how many minutes per step for beginners)

### R-04 — Sermon on the Mount Scholarship

**Goal:** Scholarly grounding for the Beatitudes section.

**Sources:**
- John Stott, *The Message of the Sermon on the Mount* (IVP, 1978)
- Craig Keener, *Commentary on the Gospel of Matthew* — Beatitudes section
- Greek word studies for each Beatitude key term (makarios, ptochos, penthountes, praeis, etc.)

**Output per beatitude:**
```
Greek key word: [word]
Transliteration: [phonetic]
Semantic range: [what this word actually means]
Cultural context: [what a 1st century hearer would understand]
Application: [100 words — what this looks like in modern life]
```

### R-05 — Transformation Testimonies

**Goal:** 6+ sourced accounts of people whose encounter with God's love changed their trajectory.

**Published accounts to use (do not fabricate):**
1. Augustine — *Confessions* Book VIII (the garden scene)
2. C.S. Lewis — *Surprised by Joy* (final conversion on a bus)
3. Francis Collins — *The Language of God* (waterfall moment)
4. Lee Strobel — *The Case for Christ* (wife's conversion first, then his)
5. Tolstoy — *A Confession* (crisis of meaning, discovery of faith)
6. Malcolm Muggeridge — *Jesus: The Man Who Lives*

**Output per testimony:**
```
Person: [name, dates]
Era: [century or approximate year]
Vignette: [2-3 sentences — the moment of change, sourced directly]
Source: [book title, chapter or page reference]
```

### R-06 — Divine Hiddenness Theology

**Goal:** Honest, warm theological treatment of why God doesn't simply appear.

**Sources:**
- Blaise Pascal, *Pensées* — the hidden God (Deus absconditus)
- Søren Kierkegaard — indirect communication and faith
- Michael Rea, *The Hiddenness of God* (OUP, 2018)
- John Hick, *Evil and the God of Love*

**Output:**
- 300-word theological explanation of divine hiddenness
- Why this is not a defeater for theism — 3 responses
- Pastoral tone: this is addressed to someone in pain, not winning a debate

### R-07 — Saints Biographies

**Goal:** Human portraits of 4 saints who radiated love in action.

**Saints to research:**
1. Francis of Assisi — G.K. Chesterton's biography recommended
2. Thérèse of Lisieux — *Story of a Soul* (direct source)
3. Mother Teresa — *Come Be My Light* (letters)
4. Maximilian Kolbe — *Saint of Auschwitz* (historical accounts)

**Output per saint:**
```
Name: [name, dates]
Tradition: [Catholic / Orthodox / etc.]
Love-in-action story: [200 words — one specific event showing love in action]
Key quote: [1 sentence, sourced]
Why they matter: [50 words — what they uniquely reveal about love]
```

### R-08 — Spiritual Disciplines

**Goal:** Taxonomy and starter practices for 12 spiritual disciplines.

**Sources:**
- Dallas Willard, *The Spirit of the Disciplines* (Harper, 1988)
- Richard Foster, *Celebration of Discipline*
- John Ortberg, *The Life You've Always Wanted*

**Output per discipline:**
```
Name: [discipline name]
Category: abstinence | engagement
Definition: [1 sentence]
Description: [100 words — what it is and why it matters]
1-week starter practice: [day-by-day or daily guidance — specific and achievable]
Scripture basis: [1 verse]
```

### R-09 — Hesychasm & Orthodox Prayer

**Goal:** Accurate introduction to the Jesus Prayer and hesychast tradition.

**Sources:**
- Philokalia Vol. 1 (especially Evagrius and John Climacus)
- *The Way of a Pilgrim* (anonymous, 19th century Russian)
- Kallistos Ware, *The Orthodox Way* (St Vladimir's Seminary Press)

**Output:**
- 400-word history of the Jesus Prayer: origins, hesychasm, Gregory Palamas's defense
- Exact method: posture, breathing, rhythm, frequency
- The theological basis: invoking the Name of Jesus as presence
- Connection to the Philokalia

### R-10 — Prayer Research Summary

**Goal:** Honest summary of scientific perspectives on prayer.

**Sources:**
- Duke MANTRA study (Mitchell Krucoff, 2001, 2005) — cite the actual results
- Larry Dossey, *Healing Words* (overview only)

**Caution:** Do not overclaim. Frame as: "Some research suggests... but results are mixed and the methodology is debated." The value of prayer is not in clinical studies but in the relationship it expresses.

**Output:**
- 200-word summary: what was studied, what the results showed, appropriate caveats
- 1 sentence: why Christians don't need this evidence but it's interesting nonetheless

### R-11 — Journey Stage Framework

**Goal:** Map the 5-stage spiritual journey to established frameworks.

**Sources:**
- James Fowler, *Stages of Faith* (Harper, 1981)
- Teresa of Avila, *The Interior Castle* (7 mansions → condense to 5 stages)
- John of the Cross — stages of the spiritual life
- Simple mapping to: Curious → Seeker → Believer → Disciple → Mystic/Saint

**Output per stage:**
```
Stage: [number and name]
Who is here: [3-sentence description of this person's inner life]
What they need: [what content/experience serves them best]
Resources: [3-5 specific content links within the platform]
Practices: [2-3 achievable practices for this stage]
Encouragement: [2 sentences written directly to this person]
```

### R-12 — 7-Day Retreat Content Plan

**Goal:** Finalize all content for the 7-Day Guided Retreat (see main plan for day-by-day outline).

**Dependencies:** R-01, R-02, R-11

**Output:** For each of the 7 days, finalize:
- Exact Scripture passage (with verse range)
- Prayer guide (type of prayer + specific instructions)
- Reflection prompt (1 open question)
- Action step (specific, achievable in 24 hours)
- Transition note (1 sentence connecting this day to the next)
